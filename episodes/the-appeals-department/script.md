# UNITED ROAD — THE APPEALS DEPARTMENT

**An original adult football comedy.** News window: 28 September–4 October 2026. Target: roughly six minutes, depending on performances and reaction holds. Strong language. All dialogue, meetings and character behaviour below are invented parody.

**The story:** Gary turns football's appetite for appeals into a business. Ronaldo demands a private hearing; Gary borrows a football crowd loophole to deliver one. His customers become pretend children, his own small print makes their appeals free, and the only actual adult in the room takes charge.

**Speaking cast:** Gary Neville, Roy Keane, Micah Richards, Cristiano Ronaldo and Pep Guardiola. Pep is a fictional invited guest, not presented as City's current manager or official representative. No narrator or additional speaking roles.

**Timing guide:** 05:34 at about 155 spoken words per minute, with 92 seconds reserved for action and reactions. About six minutes at a slower delivery. The episode has 625 spoken words and 93 lines. Director notes and repeated actor sheets are not spoken.

# Director's note for Claude — THE APPEALS DEPARTMENT

## The job

Build a roughly five-to-six-minute United Road cartoon from the supplied director script and matching actor line sheets. Use the existing United Road character likenesses and established bold-outline, large-head, flat cut-out look. The comedy should feel like a small group of selfish adults getting trapped by their own decisions: status panic, social embarrassment, bureaucracy and a calm person refusing to indulge them.

The script is an original connected episode. Its comic rhythm uses clear setups, escalating consequences, interrupted confidence and held reactions. Keep the actors' wording and the news boundaries intact. The exact measured runtime follows the recorded voices; the shot plan is an editing guide.

## Character direction

- **Gary:** Sell everything as common sense. He thinks he is the sensible professional. His eyes check the card reader when his mouth talks about fairness. Only let him become frantic after the refund chime. His final loss of dignity is physical: forced to sit beneath his own sign.
- **Roy:** Short, direct statements. He wants tea and a real football discussion. Small eye movements are enough. He enjoys the authority of supervising the others, then resumes being practical. Leave room after his lines; he does not explain his joke or look pleased for the audience.
- **Micah:** A willing accomplice who mistakes operating a button for understanding a system. Two brief laughs are enough, as nonverbal performance: after the first customer trap and after LITTLE CRIS appears. At the refund he should be sincerely pleased that he followed the instructions. He never deliberately betrays Gary.
- **Ronaldo:** Public composure over thin-skinned outrage. He changes the phone angle before changing his argument. His avoidance of the bench is one quick, precise move, not a long dance. As a pretend child he is still the existing adult cartoon character with a cheap pass. His final advice to Gary is comfortably hypocritical.
- **Pep:** Begins as an irritated interview guest. He challenges the wording, quietly reorganises the space, then wins by reading Gary's terms. Play his final discovery softly. He is an invited fictional guest, not City's current head coach or an authorised legal spokesperson.

Give each actor a different physical rhythm. Roy settles and stays still. Gary makes small promotional hand movements that get quicker under pressure. Micah reaches before thinking. Ronaldo leads with his chest and chin. Pep places objects precisely and makes a small explanatory pinch gesture.

## Camera language

Use a stable studio layout: Gary and the reader at screen left; Roy at screen right; Micah near the printer; the door and bench visible toward the centre-right. Maintain this geography when cutting between fronts and three-quarter views. All eye-lines must meet the person or prop being addressed. A reverse angle should be a deliberate set composition with consistent furniture and lighting.

Most dialogue uses medium singles or two-shots. Let a character finish the decisive part of a line before cutting to the listener. Cut to an object when that object changes the story. Hold a wide when the audience needs to see someone's failed physical action. Foreground desk edges and the curtain supply modest depth during the few camera moves.

There are four purposeful camera movements:

1. **Opening reveal:** a gentle lateral move across the respectable studio, ending on Gary's cheap sign and card reader. The professional façade visibly gives way to his business idea.
2. **Ronaldo entrance:** follow the suitcase wheel that traps the paper, then tilt/pan to reveal him. His arrival grows out of the room's existing mess.
3. **Private-hearing transition:** track the curtain edge into the improvised junior room. Land and settle before the next line.
4. **Closing reveal:** ease out from Gary on the bench into the same composition used at the start. The whole frame now shows his failure. Stop moving for the last exchange and printer switch-off.

Use a short push toward Gary when he recognises the loophole, and toward Pep when he recognises the refund. These are small emphasis moves. Camera sweeps should have a subject and a destination; stop them before the punchline so the performance can land.

## Animation and voice implementation

Use the shared cut-out character rig and separate hand shapes. Bends and reaches should use shoulder, elbow and wrist motion. A prop is parented to the holding hand at a stable grip point. The folder, phone and card reader must never hover independently of the fingers.

Keep the face aligned to one head. Clear or mask baked mouth detail before applying a replacement mouth, and remove extra facial layers that would produce a double face. Animate phoneme shapes to the recorded performance; only the person speaking has speaking-mouth motion. Listening reactions come from eye-lines, a blink, a small head change or a held pose.

Feet plant on the floor. For the bench recoil, give Ronaldo a tiny lowering anticipation, one short backward/vertical recovery, then a firm settle. For Gary's actual sit, bend at hip and knees and keep the bench in the same screen position. For exits, use a consistent short walk cycle with shoes attached to the feet. Preserve body scale through pose and angle changes.

The papers should contact the suitcase wheel and Gary's lap. The clipboard touches the desk before its sound. The final hand reaches the printer switch before it falls silent. These little causal contacts carry the comedy better than constant bouncing or zooming.
